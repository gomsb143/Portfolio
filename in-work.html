Case Study

5 → 12 user stories.
Same team. 3 months.

Before5 stories
After12 stories
Timeframe3 months
Team changeNone
The Problem

Our team was completing only 5 user stories per sprint despite having the technical capacity to deliver more. The bottleneck wasn't development — it was the delivery process. No clearly defined roadmap, misaligned project phases, and stakeholders introducing urgent changes late in the cycle. Requirements evolved after development had started, causing rework during UAT. Testing and deployment preparation started too late, creating delays and increasing release risk.

What I Changed
  • Defined a clear delivery roadmap with timelines, milestones, and approval gates before development began
  • Introduced early stakeholder demos in developers' local environments before SIT to validate functionality and catch change requests early
  • Ensured unit test, SIT, and UAT test cases along with test data were prepared during development — not after
  • Started deployment planning one week before release including checklists, approvals, environment validation, and rollback planning
What Happened
  • Sprint throughput increased from 5 to 12 completed user stories without increasing team size
  • Change requests during UAT reduced significantly through early stakeholder validation
  • Delays between Development, SIT, and UAT eliminated by preparing testing activities in advance
  • Deployment readiness improved — smoother releases with fewer last-minute issues
  • The process became the team's standard way of working and continued to improve as the team grew
What I'd Do Differently

I would introduce stakeholder demonstrations and deployment readiness reviews even earlier. I would also implement delivery metrics and dashboards from day one to monitor cycle time, bottlenecks, and throughput continuously — that visibility earlier would have allowed us to identify inefficiencies faster and optimise sooner.
